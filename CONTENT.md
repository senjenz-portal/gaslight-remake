## Why this beat, and what "much more clear" means

The reference owner's original complaint was *"i feel the story is hard to
follow."* The fix was the comprehension contract: after playing Beat I a
first-time reader must be able to state 11 facts (below). Clarity is measured
against those facts — every fact needs a CARRIER the reader cannot miss.
The remake must be at least as clear, ideally clearer: stronger affordances,
more readable staging, focus always on-frame.

## Presentation grammar (the rules of the experience)

1. Prose sits as cream serif ink in the dark page margin beside the diorama —
   edgeless wash, no boxes. ≤3 lines visible; earlier lines recede (~34%).
2. One unit = ONE speech. Prefix in small caps (HOLMES / NOTE / CLIENT / KING).
   Watson's lines carry no prefix (the reader IS Watson). A hairline leader
   connects the active speech to the speaker's head while on stage.
3. Never show text the scene performs. The note-throw, the colossus entrance,
   the mask-tear are PANTOMIME, not prose.
4. Click-paced: each unit holds its freeze frame until the reader clicks
   (breathing teal dot = the affordance). Gate units instead demand their verb
   (hold the note / click the mask / click the index / click the door).
5. Plates (close-up 2D images) are earned: camera pushes first, world dims but
   stays; the watermark plate resolves IN PROPORTION to the reader's hold.
6. Cameo card, lower-left, first appearance only; captions carry identity —
   the King's cameo FLIPS at the unmask.
7. The completing action turns the page (the door click ends Beat I).
8. Audio is diegetic only — beds + moment cues, no music.

## The 11 facts (sign-off checklist for the "user" lens)

I.1 someone unknown, masked, comes tonight · I.2 paper's watermark → Bohemia ·
I.3 the masked man IS the note's author · I.4 he is enormous, masked, in the
room · I.5 he is royal (House of Ormstein) · I.6 he is the KING, desperate ·
I.7 the other party is Irene Adler · I.8 a photograph of them BOTH exists;
five recovery attempts failed · I.9 she'll use it to destroy his marriage ·
I.10 deadline Monday; address Briony Lodge, Serpentine Avenue · I.11 Holmes
takes the case; the reader's door-click is the go-decision.
