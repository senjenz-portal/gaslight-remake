## Sol's verdict (verbatim)

Overall, the characters usually read as 3D pieces placed over the paintings, not inhabitants of them. The main causes are mismatched sharpness, weak contact shadows, absent local light, exposed guide lines, and inconsistent scale cues. The wide shots integrate better because the characters are smaller and the plate’s visual register hides more of the mismatch.

## Top 8 fixes, ranked by reader impact

1. **III-08 — Recompose around one readable action.** Remove or heavily crop back the purple foreground figure, show more of the giant’s connected body, and give the small hero an isolated silhouette. At present the frame has no stable subject.

2. **All frames, especially I-07 and II-05 — Remove every path, control and guide overlay.** These lines turn the image from an illustrated world into a development viewport instantly.

3. **II-05, III-08, IV-03 — Make fire light affect the characters.** Add warm key light, sharp orange edge light, and darker opposing sides. The nearby fires currently illuminate only the painted plate.

4. **I-07 through V-05 — Add painted contact shadows and ambient occlusion at every foot/body contact.** Shadows should inherit the plate’s softness, color and perspective. This is the fastest way to stop hovering.

5. **I-07, II-05, III-08 — Match the character render register to each plate.** Reduce edge sharpness and fine detail, soften specular response, add plate-matched grain, and compress color/contrast. Do this after compositing, not only in the 3D render.

6. **III-08 and IV-03 — Fix giant/hero scale through spatial evidence.** Show the giant contacting the same floor, cast a shadow across the hero, and use deliberate overlap. Giant size alone does not establish distance; it currently resembles layered stickers.

7. **IV-03 — Separate the hero, fire and giant silhouette.** Move the hero’s visible head away from the torso edge and offset the fire so it does not emerge from the body. Preserve a clean outline around the giant’s face and hands.

8. **V-05 and VI-03 — Clarify the story beat with eyelines and contrast.** In V-05, make the giant and pig visibly attend to one another and suppress the fire as a competing focal point. In VI-03, selectively light or enlarge the important shipboard figure if the frame is meant to carry character information.

## Summary (3 lines)

Sol's core verdict: the 3D cast reads as stickers on top of the plates, not inhabitants — the dominant causes are missing contact shadows, no fire/local lighting on characters, render register (sharpness/saturation) far above the plate, and visible path/guide overlays.
Worst frame is III-08 (lookhere): no stable subject, floating giant feet, three incompatible lighting treatments; best integration is V-05 (dawn) where the low-poly plate register is closest to the characters, and VI-03 works only as an establishing shot.
Highest-leverage fixes: recompose III-08, strip all debug/path overlays everywhere, make fires actually light the characters, and paint plate-matched contact shadows at every foot contact.
