The Living Plate

One master plate — 221B, gas-lit, on its floating rock — and two ways to make it move without ever ceasing to be itself. Nothing here is re-composed, re-lit or re-modelled. The brief for both techniques was the same: the plate stays the plate; only the air, the fire and the lamplight are allowed to change.

the plate, breathed

technique one · generative video

The plate handed to an image-to-video model as both the first frame and the last frame, so the clip is forced to return to its own opening image. No audio, no cut. The prompt asked for light and nothing else: the lamp flame, the hearth, the window panes breathing, and a two-percent camera push. The last twelve frames are then fused into the first twelve offline, which closes the wrap exactly — one file, one <video loop>, nothing for the browser to keep in sync.

0.27 mean, 0.27 % of pixels — against 0.13 for an ordinary adjacent pair mid-clip. The wrap is now within noise of a normal frame step.
why not js
the two-copy cross-fade has to hold its copies half a cycle apart to cover the wrap, so the blend mixes a bright hearth with a dim one for the whole clip — a permanent double exposure. Fusing the ends offline blends frames that are already near-identical: worst ghost in the window, 1.4 RGB.
attempt 1
asked for drifting fog and got a glowing ribbon orbiting the island — a structural addition. Fog was cut from the prompt entirely and moved to technique two.

the plate, layered

technique two · animated illustration

The same plate cut into depth — void, room, rock, lamp — and put back together with a twelve-second camera drift. The gas-lamp bloom was lifted out as its own additive layer before cutting, so it can pulse on its own and can never tear at a seam. Fog is a displaced turbulence band drifting between the rock and the room; the hearth, the window panes and the flame are emissive overlays. No dependencies, no video, no canvas — five images and a transform.

And because it is only images and a transform, it can take a hand. The pointer leans on the drift instead of replacing it; the lamp, the hearth and the window are diegetic targets, each wearing the book's breathing teal dot; and the window takes the book's press-and-hold — the reveal verb, resolving in proportion to the hold and springing back if you let it go.

4 depth layers · 12 s drift · pointer parallax · click the lamp, click the hearth, hold the window · 736 KB of PNG, no dependencies
/
the void
the backdrop is an isotropic radial gradient about (700, 390); a quadratic-in-r fit recovers it to 2.7 RGB RMS, which gives an exact silhouette by residual instead of a traced outline
the bloom
the lamp halo measured as an additive radial profile in the clean void sector, subtracted from the plate, shipped as its own screen-blended layer
the cuts
two hand-authored polygons — the lamp column, and the deck-to-rock rim — intersected with that silhouette
the fill
26 px of harmonically inpainted headroom behind every layer, so the drift never opens a hole
parallax
room 0.34 · rock 0.60 · lamp 1.00, ±11 px on an ellipse — a diorama turning, not cards sliding
the pointer
the pointer does not drive the camera, it biases it: a ±26 px offset added to the same ellipse at each layer's own depth, eased in over about two seconds so a flicked mouse never snaps the diorama. Take the pointer away and the bias decays to zero and the idle drift is all that is left. Withheld entirely under prefers-reduced-motion.
the verbs
three things on the plate answer to the hand, each with the book's breathing teal dot standing on it: the lamp flares (halo brightens, flicker quickens, a warm pool spreads for 3 s), the hearth stokes (the emissive's pulse amplitude and rate up for 4 s), and the window takes a press-and-hold — the reveal verb, resolving in proportion to the hold, with the book's progress ring at the pointer and an elastic fall-back if you let go early.

the plate as the page

the book's verbs on a living plate

The stage above proves the plate can hold a hand. This proves it can hold the book: three units of Beat I, Doyle verbatim, set as cream serif ink in the dark page margin beside a living diorama — one speech per unit, prefixed in small caps, the reader click-paced by a breathing teal dot, earlier lines receding so the eye always lands on the newest. Nothing here is a widget: the affordance is type in the same margin, and the room bed lights up on your first gesture.

the text
units 1–3 of CONTENT.md — post, undated, note1 — transcribed, not paraphrased. A thing read is not a thing said: the note is ruled off and set in italic, and it carries no speaker's voice.
the pacing
click-paced, never timed. Three blocks maximum in the column, older ones at 62 % so the column never scrolls and the live line always leads.
the audio
diegetic only — room-bed under everything from the first gesture, paper-rustle on the note-throw, click-soft on every advance. No music.

the actors step out of the painting

the plate is a stage · now the cast moves

The layered plate proved the room is a stage. This proves the figures on it are actors. Holmes has been cut out of the plate into his own alpha, the wall and hearth behind him painted back in, and the cut-out re-hung on the stage as five hinged pieces — head, near arm, torso, gown, legs — over the room he used to be part of. He breathes and turns towards the window on his own; click him and he draws on the pipe; click the desk and he walks to it as a four-frame sprite, passing behind Watson's armchair, which is a second cut-out laid in front of him. Watson is left baked into the plate on purpose: one actor is enough to prove the technique, and the difference between the two of them is the whole point.

1 actor · 5 hinged parts · 4-frame walk · 2 verbs · the chair in front of him is a cut-out too
/
the cut
a hand polygon brackets him; the alpha inside it is not traced, it is measured — the plate diffed against a generative fill of the same crop with the figure removed. Two tests have to agree: the pixel changed, and no small offset of the fill explains it (a fill re-draws unchanged geometry a pixel or two over, and a naive diff calls that a man). One colour veto handles the fire-lit post of the fireplace beside him, which no diff can tell from him.
the inpaint
the fill is trusted only inside the polygon: the paste is masked and feathered to it, so the diff against the original plate is 0 changed pixels outside the figure's own region. Outside the polygon the model's own agreement with the plate is 9.0 RGB RMS — which is why it is not the thing that ships there.
the parts
five claims resolved into a strict partition, each grown one pixel into its neighbour. Parts that sum to the figure do not composite to it — two 50 % edges stack to 75 % and the seam reads as a crack of daylight — so they overlap instead: invisible at rest, and it is the pixel that holds the join closed when they swing apart.
the idle
sim-time sinusoids on nested transforms, no keyframes and no library: breath on the chest at 4.1 s, the gown at 7.7 s, the arm at 5.9 s, and a 13 s turn of the head towards the window. Rotations stay under 3° because a painted cut-out forgives 3° and not 8°.
the walk
the same painted Holmes, redrawn as a four-frame strip by image-to-image from his own cut-out, keyed off flat green, then normalised: one height (the four came back 1.5 % apart), one foot baseline, torso centred, and the palette pulled 65 % back to the cut-out's own mean and deviation, because i2i drifts warm and he has to match the room he is walking across.
the occlusion
Watson's armchair is genuinely in the foreground, so it was cut the same way and re-laid above the actor in DOM order — no z-index anywhere in the stack. Because its opaque pixels are the plate's own, compositing it over the plate changes 0.0000 RGB on average and 0 pixels by more than 6: the layer costs nothing at all until somebody walks behind it.
the headroom
a cut-out puppet has nothing behind its own arm — swing it and the fireplace shows through the hole it left. So every part is extended 14 px under the parts that cover it and those pixels are harmonically filled from the part's own paint, seeded so the Laplacian cannot reach round the silhouette and drag the room in with it. The gown continues behind the sleeve, so the sleeve has somewhere to go.
the floor
the two marks are read off the plate — his own shoe base at the hearth, and a spot at the desk by the window. The room's floor is planar, so the line between them is the floor line; the sprite's foot baseline rides it, and depth eases the scale from 1.00 to 0.885 across the room. The verifier measures that against the rendered box, not against the numbers that drew it: worst deviation across a walk, 0.5 px.

Which of the two actually holds up. The puppet is the one to keep. It is the plate's own paint, so it cannot drift: at rest it is the plate, and the idle only ever asks for a degree or two, which is exactly what a painted cut-out will give you. It is also the cheap one — 42 KB of parts and no new pixels invented anywhere a viewer can look. What it cannot do is change pose: he can breathe and turn and raise the pipe, and that is the whole vocabulary. The sprite buys real locomotion and pays for it in identity — the four frames came back from i2i a percent and a half apart in height and noticeably warmer than the plate, and even after normalising and pulling the palette back he is a redrawing of Holmes rather than Holmes. At this scale, mid- stride, that is a fair trade; hold a walk frame still next to the cut-out and it is not. The honest shape of the technique is what is built here: puppet for everything that happens on the mark, sprite only for the crossing between marks, and the hand-off hidden in the one moment the eye is following movement rather than reading a face.

the second plate, breathed

the same trick, the street
5.5 s loop · 900 × 900 · fused seam 0.02

The street corner ran through the identical pipeline. It holds the composition even harder than the room does — carriage, horses and every figure stay exactly put — but it has less to burn: two lamps and a wall of windows, no hearth. The room plate is the better subject for this treatment, and it is the one the layered build was cut from.

Its first attempt failed the same way the room's did, and worse: a prompt that mentioned fog produced a white plume rolling off the cobbles and blew out the carriage lantern halfway through.

which one reads better

measured, not argued

Both stages were captured through the same box, ten frames apart, and each compared against its own still-plate toggle — so the two numbers below are measured through identical scaling and cropping. the video moves better
Twice the motion energy, and it is the right motion: the hearth flickers with a changing flame shape rather than a pulsing ellipse, the window panes brighten unevenly pane by pane, and the push-in is a real dolly, so every edge in the frame gets correct perspective — not three cards on three speeds. Nothing was authored to get it.

the layers stay the plate
12.25 is not a light change. The model re-framed the plate to 16:9 — its diorama sits about 7 % larger in the box than the original does — and repainted it on the way: mantel clutter, rock facets, wall tint. No frame of the loop is ever the plate again. The layered build is the plate — its 3.53 is the fog and the emissives, added light and nothing else. It is also deterministic, re-cuttable onto any plate by the same script, tunable (parallax, fog, flicker are all parameters), reduced-motion aware, and it can take a pointer.

The layered build is the one to carry. The direction was that the art is the experience and the plate must stay the plate; a technique that quietly repaints the art fails that on its own terms, however lovely the light. The video earns a place as the hero moment — a single loop at the top of a page, where nobody is holding it against the original. The layered build is what the product should be made of, and the one thing it lacks is depth inside the room: one more cut, separating the two wall planes from the furniture standing on the floor, would give the walls their own parallax and close most of the gap to the video's dolly.
